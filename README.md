# Does a 1586 siting decision still decide who floods? The Tura at Tyumen, 2026

Independent research project by Sasha Ostriagin (Bromsgrove School, UK):
a satellite flood-mapping analysis of the July–August 2026 Tura flood at
Tyumen, western Siberia.

**Read the report:** [`report/Ostriagin_2026_Tura_flood_Tyumen.pdf`](report/Ostriagin_2026_Tura_flood_Tyumen.pdf)

**Research question.** Tyumen was founded in 1586 on the high southern bank
of the Tura; the northern bank is low. Does that siting decision still
determine which side of the river floods — and did the 2026 flood, a record
for a summer flood at 897 cm on 2 August (the all-time record remains the
spring 1979 flood at 915 cm), show that the old siting logic holds only up to
a threshold?

**Headline result.** Mapped from Sentinel-2, the flood covered 415 ha (137 ha
before it). 120 of 716 buildings flooded on the low north bank (1 in 6),
against 18 of 4,437 on the high south bank (1 in 250).

## Reproducing the raw data

The `data/raw/` folder is not committed (large binary files, and it is fully
reproducible). To rebuild it:

1. **Sentinel-2**: in the Copernicus Browser, search tile T41VPD / Tyumen for
   18 June 2026 and 29 July 2026, and export bands B02/B03/B04/B11 (Analytical
   download) to `data/raw/s2_20260618/` and `data/raw/s2_20260729/` respectively.
2. **DEM**: download, to `data/raw/` (public, unauthenticated):
   `https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N57_00_E065_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N57_00_E065_00_DEM.tif`
   and, for the Shrewsbury comparison,
   `https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N52_00_W003_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N52_00_W003_00_DEM.tif`
   With both tiles in place, `python scripts/extract_transects.py` regenerates both
   cross-section profiles from scratch. The committed
   `data/processed/shrewsbury_cross_section_profile.npz` was produced by that same
   routine (bilinear, 5 m spacing, 181 points); the working environment could not
   download the whole tile, so its pixels were read over HTTP range requests against
   the same public object. Running the script with the tile present reproduces it.
3. **OSM**: `curl "https://overpass-api.de/api/map?bbox=65.50,57.14,65.56,57.17" -o data/raw/osm_tyumen_extract.osm`

## Method summary

1. `scripts/dem_water_mask.py` — computes MNDWI = (B3−B11)/(B3+B11) for both
   dates, reprojects the DEM onto the Sentinel-2 grid, and derives a
   DEM-constrained water mask (spectral threshold + elevation cut-off +
   connectivity to a high-confidence seed) to remove cloud-shadow and
   dark-rooftop false positives that a pure spectral threshold picks up in
   this urban scene.
2. `scripts/polygonize_and_exposure.py` — polygonises the masks, derives a
   north/south dividing line from the local river tangent (calibrated
   against the known south-bank location of the Tyumen kremlin), and counts
   flooded buildings, roads and (via the WorldPop API) population on each
   bank.
3. `scripts/make_fig1_context.py`, `scripts/make_fig2_flood_extent.py`,
   `scripts/make_fig3_cross_section.py`, `scripts/make_fig4_exposure.py` —
   produce the four report figures.
4. `scripts/build_qgis_project.py` — writes `qgis/tura_flood_tyumen.qgs`
   referencing the processed vector and raster layers.

Run in order after reproducing the raw data (`data/raw/`); each script reads
from and writes to `data/processed/` and (for figures) `figures/`.

## Requirements

Python 3, with `rasterio`, `geopandas` (pyogrio backend), `shapely`,
`scipy`, `matplotlib`, `numpy`. Report built with `pandoc` (xelatex engine,
for Cyrillic text support).

## Limitations

See `report/Ostriagin_2026_Tura_flood_Tyumen.pdf` §Limitations — in brief: 10–20 m pixel
size, single-overpass snapshot two days before the observed peak (likely a
conservative/lower-bound flood extent), and residual under/over-counting
from cloud shadow and the DEM-connectivity approximation (~3–4% area
change from the geometry simplification used for the WorldPop query).
